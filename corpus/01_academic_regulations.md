# Nexus University Academic Regulations
## Academic Year 2026-2027

Nexus University publishes these Academic Regulations for undergraduate and postgraduate programmes offered on the main campus. They bind every registered student unless a programme-specific handbook states a narrower rule that has been approved by the Academic Council. Where a programme handbook is silent, this document controls. Students are expected to read this file together with attendance, examination, fee, hostel, discipline, and scholarship policies.

## Section 1.1 Scope and Authority

These regulations apply to all credit-bearing courses taught during Academic Year 2026-27, including summer term courses that carry credit toward a Nexus University award. Guest workshops that do not carry credit are excluded. The Academic Council is the final academic authority. The Registrar maintains the official student record. Faculties may issue operational circulars, but a circular cannot create a new degree requirement unless the Academic Council ratifies it. Students who enrol after the start of a semester remain bound by the regulations in force on the date of enrolment.

The University distinguishes between academic rules, which concern teaching and assessment, and administrative rules, which concern fees, identity, and campus services. Academic rules in this file take precedence over informal advice given by peers, social media posts, or unofficial summaries. Only written communication from the Registrar, Dean, or Controller of Examinations constitutes official academic notice.

## Section 1.2 Definitions

In these regulations, "student" means a person whose name appears on the current enrolment roll. "Programme" means a structured set of courses leading to a named award. "Course" means a unit of teaching with a unique course code. "Semester" means the odd term beginning in July or the even term beginning in January, as published in the academic calendar. "Internal assessment" means marks awarded for work completed before the end-semester examination. "Working day" excludes University holidays listed in the calendar. "Identity card" means the official Nexus University smart card issued by the Office of Student Services.

## Section 1.3 Academic Calendar and Registration

The Academic Calendar for 2026-2027 is published on the student portal not later than 15 May 2026. Course registration opens ten working days before instruction begins and closes on the Friday of the second teaching week. Late registration, if permitted by the Head of Department, attracts the late-registration fee published in the Fee Policy. Students who fail to register by the close of week three are treated as not enrolled for that semester and may not attend classes or sit examinations. Add-and-drop is allowed only during the first two teaching weeks. After that window, withdrawal from a course is recorded as W on the transcript if completed before the mid-semester test, or as F if attempted later without approved medical or academic leave.

## Section 1.4 Programme Duration and Credit Load

The standard duration of a four-year undergraduate programme is eight semesters. The maximum duration is twelve semesters from first enrolment, after which the student is discontinued unless the Academic Council grants an exceptional extension for documented medical or military service reasons. A full-time undergraduate student shall register for not fewer than sixteen and not more than twenty-six credits in a regular semester. Overload above twenty-six credits requires a cumulative grade point average of at least 8.0 and written approval of the Head of Department. Credit from recognised exchange programmes may be transferred only if the Learning Agreement was approved before departure.

## Section 1.5 Grading System

Nexus University uses a ten-point grade point scale. Letter grades and grade points are: O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7), B (6), C (5), P (4), and F (0). Internal assessment normally contributes forty percent of the course total and the end-semester examination contributes sixty percent, unless the course file approved by the Board of Studies states a studio or laboratory pattern. A student must obtain at least forty percent in the end-semester examination and at least forty percent in the overall course total to earn a pass grade. Relative grading may be used in large first-year courses if announced in the first week. Incomplete grades (I) must be converted within the following semester or they become F.

Grade reports are released through the student portal. Clerical errors may be corrected on application within fifteen days of publication. Requests for re-totaling of end-semester scripts follow the Examination Rules. There is no provision in these Academic Regulations for changing a letter grade on compassionate grounds, nor for awarding a pass by committee vote where the student has not met the published assessment requirements.

## Section 1.6 Internal Assessment Components

Internal assessment typically comprises a mid-semester test, at least one assignment or laboratory record, and class participation or quizzes, as stated in the course plan. Instructors shall publish the component weights in the first week. Students who miss a mid-semester test without approved leave receive zero for that component. Make-up mid-semester tests are granted only for University duty, approved medical exemption, or bereavement of an immediate family member, and must be completed before the start of end-semester examinations. Assignments submitted after the published deadline lose ten percent of the assignment marks per working day, up to a maximum of five working days, after which the assignment is not accepted.

Group assignments require a contribution statement. If a team member is found not to have contributed, that member may receive a reduced mark without automatically penalising contributing members. Plagiarism in assignments is handled under academic misconduct rules in the Student Discipline policy. Recycling of a student's own previously submitted work without citation is treated as unacknowledged reuse and may attract a mark penalty.

## Section 1.7 Academic Standing and Progression

A student remains in good academic standing if the semester grade point average is at least 5.0 and the cumulative grade point average is at least 5.0. A student whose semester grade point average falls below 5.0 is placed on academic warning. Two consecutive warnings result in academic probation. A student on probation must meet the Head of Department, limit extracurricular office-holding, and follow a reduced-credit plan if advised. Failure to raise the cumulative grade point average above 5.0 after two probation semesters may lead to discontinuation from the programme after a hearing before the Faculty Academic Committee.

Progression to the next year of study requires the student to have earned the minimum credits listed in the programme structure, including all listed core courses of the previous year or an approved equivalent. Carrying more than three backlogs into the final year is not permitted except by Dean's waiver after a documented review of remaining degree requirements.

## Section 1.8 Identity Cards, Library, and Campus Access

Every student shall carry the University identity card while on campus and produce it when asked by authorised staff. The card is required to enter the library, hostels, examination halls, and laboratories after hours. Loss of a card must be reported to Student Services within twenty-four hours. A duplicate card is issued on payment of the published replacement fee. Lending an identity card to another person is a disciplinary offence.

The Central Library is open from 8:00 AM to 10:00 PM on teaching days and from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM on Sundays, except University holidays. Undergraduate students may borrow up to four print volumes for fourteen days. Overdue charges apply as posted at the circulation desk. Food, loud conversation, and unauthorised photography of rare collections are prohibited. Remote access to licensed electronic resources is limited to current members and must not be shared outside the University.

## Section 1.9 Student Committees and Representation

Each department shall maintain a Student Academic Committee with elected student members from each year of study. The committee may recommend improvements to timetables, laboratory access, and academic support, but it cannot alter grading schemes or attendance rules. University-wide student representation occurs through the Nexus Student Council, whose constitution is approved by the Dean of Student Affairs. Office-bearers must remain in good academic standing. Committees shall keep minutes and may not collect unofficial fees.

## Section 1.10 Leave from Academic Activities

Academic leave of up to five working days in a semester may be granted by the Head of Department for University representation in approved competitions, conferences, or official duty. Leave does not by itself reduce the attendance denominator unless the circular granting leave states that the absence is duty leave. Personal travel, family functions that are not bereavement, and internships that were not pre-approved as credit-bearing do not qualify as academic leave under this section. Medical leave is processed under the Medical Exemption Rules issued as a separate document.

## Section 1.11 Branch or Programme Change

A change of undergraduate branch, where vacancies exist, may be considered only after the first year and only on the basis of first-year cumulative grade point average and availability. Applications close on the date announced by the Dean of Academic Affairs, ordinarily before the start of the third semester. Lateral movement between unrelated postgraduate programmes is not permitted.

## Section 1.12 Interpretation

Doubts about interpretation of these Academic Regulations shall be referred to the Registrar. If two academic documents appear to address the same requirement differently, the matter shall be placed before the Academic Council. Until the Council decides, instructors shall apply the stricter published academic standard for assessment integrity, while students may seek written clarification. Informal oral assurances are not a defence against a published rule.
